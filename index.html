Buyer split: John Dawes · Starting ratio: Binet & Field · How far it moves: our rule of thumb

How to judge each side

Judge each side on its own job.

Brand Building
  • Reach in your target audience
  • Household penetration (HHP), the same number you entered in step 1
  • Branded search and share of search
  • Brand awareness, prompted and unprompted
Performance
  • ROAS and incremental ROAS
  • Cost per acquisition
  • Conversion rate
  • Retail media sales
  • Purchase frequency and spend per trip among your buyers

Overall: total sales ÷ total marketing spend (marketing efficiency ratio). It shows whether the mix as a whole is working.

How it's calculated

Show method

  1. Find how often the category is bought

    Interpurchase time = 365 days ÷ Purchase Frequency.

  2. Find the share of buyers in-market

    John Dawes' 95:5 math: the share in-market is the buying window as a share of the time between purchases. Dawes uses a quarter for B2B, where buying takes about that long: if buyers switch every 5 years, about 5% are in-market in any quarter. In CPG the buying window is the decision period, so the same math gives the share deciding right now.

    in-market % = decision period ÷ interpurchase time

  3. Set the buyer split

    Out-of-market buyers are reached by Brand Building, in-market buyers by Performance. The buyer split is the upper limit for Brand Building, not the budget.

  4. Set the budget

    Dawes gives no budget split. The Binet & Field benchmark is the budget expression of 95:5: in-market buyers are fewer, but each costs more to win, so Performance gets more than its share of buyers. The starting point is your sector's optimum from Binet & Field (60:40 if no sector is chosen). From there, the out-of-market share moves it on a sliding scale: at 90% it stays at the starting point; toward 100% it leans up to 5 points further into Brand Building, while share of voice, branded search and awareness keep rising; toward 80% or below, Performance takes up to 5 points more. With no sector chosen, the lean is up to 10 points. The sliding scale is our rule of thumb, not part of Dawes' or Binet & Field's research. The advice then depends on the gap: within the range, hold; up to 5 points, a reasonable gap; 6 to 15 points, close it this planning cycle; more than 15, move 10 to 15 points first, measure share of search, share of voice and market share, and step again. Test each step with MMM and incrementality.
